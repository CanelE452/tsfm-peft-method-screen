# Figure captions — tail-complexity validity study (TCV-1)

English captions for the contracted figures. Every number below is computed by `figures.py` from the CSVs in this directory; the plotted coordinates are exported to `FIGURE_VALUES.csv`. The primary metric is G*_.995 = QS_0.995(arm with the SEL extension) / QS_0.995(reference) - 1, so 0 means the arm matches its reference and positive values mean it is worse. Hypothesis decisions are not recomputed here; they live in the S1/S2 hypothesis files.

## Figure 1 — `fig1_s1_condition_gap`

Quantile-score gap of the two forecasting arms at the out-of-grid level tau = 0.995, by synthetic condition. Bars are medians over the series of each condition (40 series per condition); whiskers span the interquartile range; the horizontal line at 0 marks parity with the oracle reference of that condition. Conditions are grouped by the autoregressive coefficient and ordered by innovation tail weight and by the volatility switch. For F0 (zero-shot), the condition medians run from 0.019 (t(6), GARCH off) to 0.114 (Gaussian, GARCH on). For LoRA, the condition medians run from 0.015 (Gaussian, GARCH off) to 0.107 (t(3.5), GARCH on). The comparison is within condition: each arm is scored against the reference of its own condition, so bar heights measure relative loss, not absolute difficulty.

## Figure 2a — `fig2_xi_vs_gap_s1`

Estimated tail index of the training window against the out-of-grid gap G*_.995. Each point is one synthetic series (480 per arm); colour encodes the generating condition. Filled circles are the frozen arm and open triangles the LoRA arm, drawn on the same axes; the two straight lines are ordinary least-squares fits of the gap on xi-hat (solid: frozen, dashed: LoRA). F0 (zero-shot): Spearman rho = -0.014, Pearson r = 0.012, least-squares slope = 0.010 per unit of xi-hat, n = 480; LoRA: Spearman rho = 0.055, Pearson r = 0.066, least-squares slope = 0.056 per unit of xi-hat, n = 480. The horizontal line at 0 marks parity with the reference. These are unconditional associations on the raw gap; the contracted regressions model log(1 + G*_.995) with spectral entropy and the extremal index included and are reported separately.

## Figure 3 — `fig3_matched_pairs_se_xi`

Spectrum-matched pairs of synthetic series: within one autoregressive coefficient, each series generated under the baseline condition (Gaussian, GARCH off) is paired with the same series index under a heavier-tail or volatility-switched condition. The axes are the within-pair differences of spectral entropy (x) and of the estimated tail index (y), n = 400 pairs. Colour encodes the matched contrast and marker fill the autoregressive coefficient; the cross marks the mean over all pairs with a percentile-bootstrap 95% interval (2000 draws, seed 20260924; pairs that share a series index are not independent). Mean difference in spectral entropy is -0.0004 (95% CI -0.0034 to 0.0025), while the mean difference in the tail index is 0.2808 (95% CI 0.2643 to 0.2971), with 94.5% of pairs above the horizontal zero line. For scale, the spectral entropy of these series itself runs from 0.637 to 0.882, while the largest absolute within-pair difference is 0.1205. Points therefore concentrate on a vertical band around zero on the x axis and sit above zero on the y axis: the second-order spectrum is held while the tail shape moves, which is the non-redundancy the design requires.
